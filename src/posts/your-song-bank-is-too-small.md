Let me define a term first, because the whole post hangs on it.

Your song bank is the number of songs you can lead from memory, today, without a phone in your hand. Not the songs you have heard. Not the songs you once knew. The songs you could start right now, in a sensible key, and carry to the end.

Most lead singers have never counted theirs. When they do, the number is smaller than they expected.

## The old belief: "The Spirit will give me songs"

I have heard it many times, and I understand where it comes from. You are standing in front of the church, the atmosphere shifts, and you trust God to lead you. That trust is right. The problem is what people do with it. They stop preparing, because they believe the songs will simply arrive on the day.

In the Handbook I wrote something I still stand on: even if the Holy Spirit will light upon you and inspire songs out of you in a session, He will most likely operate with the level of depth you have acquired, technically and spiritually.

Permit me to speak a bit bluntly. A small song bank is a limit you place on the session before it even starts. Calling it humility does not change that.

Think of a buka that cooks only one soup. On the first day, the customers are happy. By the second week, they have found the woman down the road with 5 pots. The first woman did not lose her customers because her soup was bad. She lost them because she had nothing else to offer when they wanted something different.

Scripture describes the kind of store a worshipper should carry:

> "Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly in all wisdom; teaching and admonishing one another in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, singing with grace in your hearts to the Lord." (Colossians 3:16)

Dwell. It lives there, it is not visiting. Richly. Not a small portion. Psalms and hymns and spiritual songs. More than one kind. The verse pictures a person so full of songs that they overflow into teaching and encouraging others. You cannot pour out what was never put in.

## The song bank rule

I call it **the song bank rule**: you can only lead from what you have deposited, and you can only withdraw what you have kept.

There are 2 parts to that. Deposit, which is learning new songs. Keep, which is making sure you can still sing them in a month. Most singers fail at one or the other, and the reasons usually come down to 3 things.

### 1. You only learn songs in your own style

Most singers prefer one sound, one pattern, sometimes one language. That is not wrong. Depth in one area matters. But in the Handbook I wrote that not every environment will flow with your preferred style and sound, and that versatility increases the range of options you make available to the Holy Spirit during a session.

Picture a DJ at an owambe who only brought one kind of music. The aunties want highlife, the young people want Afrobeats, the old men want juju. He plays his favourite playlist all night and wonders why the dance floor is empty. He is a good DJ. He just came with too narrow a bag.

The fix I gave in the Handbook is simple: set a daily goal to learn one new song, and make it a style you are not used to. It does not have to take more than 10 minutes. Learn the chorus and the vital parts. Do that for a month and you have 30 new songs.

### 2. You learn songs but you do not keep them

Many singers learn a song on Tuesday, sing it on Sunday and lose it by the next month. They deposited, but they never kept.

In the Handbook I wrote that you should record every song you learn and revisit your recordings weekly. Do not keep them only in your head. Many new songs have been lost through carelessness in how people stored them. A folder on your phone, named and dated, is a song bank you can actually check.

Think of a phone with 2,000 contacts saved as "New", "New 2" and "Bros". The numbers are all there, but when you need one in a hurry, you cannot find it. A recording called "Audio 0347" is the same. Name every song as you save it.

### 3. You cram the night before

This is the one most of us are guilty of. Saturday night, you discover you are leading on Sunday, so you sit with a song for 2 hours until you have it. On Sunday you sing it fine. 3 weeks later it is gone.

Research on song memory gives a useful picture here. A study at York University in Canada taught 87 university students, most of them music students, an unaccompanied 2-verse song until they could sing the words almost perfectly. They then practised it again after a gap of 10 minutes, 2 days or 1 week, and were tested 3 weeks later. The researchers found that spacing the practice helped people keep the song, as long as the gap passed a certain point. Waiting a full week instead of a couple of days did not add any further benefit.

Be careful with this. It is one study, with students, on one short song. A separate study from the same university, teaching a short piano sequence, found no spacing benefit at all. So I present this as a helpful habit, not a law. But the lesson is practical: learning a song once, in one long session, is the weakest way to keep it. Come back to it a couple of days later, then again the next week.

## Now let me establish balance

I am not telling you to stuff your head with 500 songs and lead like a jukebox. A big song bank with no understanding of the songs is still shallow. Know what each song is saying, so you can carry its message with your face and your voice.

I am also not saying preparation replaces the Spirit's leading. I have led over 1,000 live sessions, and there are moments when a song comes that nobody planned. Those moments are real. They simply tend to come to people who have done the work.

Do we understand this? Preparation does not compete with the Holy Spirit. It gives Him more to work with.

> **The Spirit can lead you anywhere, but you can only walk Him through the songs you carry.**

## Do it now

**1. Count your song bank (15 minutes, today).** Write down every song you can lead from memory right now, from start to finish, without your phone. Do not count songs you would need to check. Write the total at the top.

**2. Deposit one song a day (10 minutes, daily for 7 days).** Each day, learn the chorus and the main verse of one song from a style or language you do not usually lead. Record yourself singing it.

**3. Keep what you deposit (5 minutes, every 2 to 3 days).** Play back your new recordings and sing along without looking at the words. Mark any song you could not finish.

**4. Test it on Sunday (2 minutes, before the service).** Pick one of your new songs and sing it through once from memory, quietly, before you go out.

**Your proof:** your song bank total from today and again in 7 days, plus 7 dated recordings in a "Song Bank" folder on your phone.
